# Brand identity: whose work this is

Read this before you think about an idea. A direction is one reading of it, not a layout.

**For a design-studio brief:** quote the block below verbatim under "The brand: whose work this is". It already includes Hung's decisions (shadows, blur, purple, green and blue, proportion, screenshots), so no override list is needed for those. Constants go in from [brand-kit.md](brand-kit.md), the frame snippet from [frames.md](frames.md).

---

> **Modern, clean, hi-tech; dark navy dominant, green as the sole signal.**
>
> Built for data people: restraint, dark navy dominant, green used so sparingly that every green element carries meaning, and depth from layering, hairline contrast and soft light rather than heavy effects. Navy owns most of a graphic: the atmosphere of a live data dashboard at night. Navy is never flat; it always has light in it (a brand background). Light graphics are never pure white.
>
> **Green is signal, not decoration.** It marks the one thing that matters: the CTA, the focal connector, the key number, the highlighted code line, the emphasized headline phrase or table column. A green headline phrase behaves like a highlighted keyword in an IDE: *this is the core value.* If green is on more than the signal, it stops registering.
>
> **Colour roles.** Green and blue are interchangeable for structure in illustrations and glyphs (green-100 → green-600, blue-100 → blue-600); green stays the focal signal. Purple appears in three places only: persona/role badges, the brand cursor, and code syntax keywords. It is not an illustration accent. A good balance is roughly 60% navy, 20% green, under 5% each of purple and blue, the rest white and gray: a preference, not a quota.
>
> **Illustrations are custom, flat, geometric forms drawn per concept**, not a fixed set of templates. The construction grammar:
> - **Flat, geometric, rounded** forms. No skeuomorphism, no realistic shading, no 3D.
> - **Ramp depth**: build and shade with one ramp, lighter = further or lower (the database glyph stacks discs green-600 → green-400 → green-200 → green-100).
> - **White hairline separators** between stacked planes.
> - **One soft directional gradient** for volume on the focal shape (the person glyph: green-500 → green-300). One light source, not on every element.
>
> **Depth and light.** Layering, overlap, ghosting and hairlines first. **Soft shadows are allowed** for depth and elevation: they follow the one light direction and lift a layer off what's behind it; no heavy or dramatic drop shadows. The forms stay flat; shadows fall *from* them. **Lens blur is allowed rarely**: at most one blurred plane, only when distance is part of the idea. Never the depth system, never a stand-in for detail that wasn't drawn.
>
> **Showing the product.** Simplify: keep the recognizable structure, drop detail that isn't in the story. Ghost and bleed: push supporting panels to monochrome or low opacity behind the focal, let panels run off an edge for depth and "there's more". A cropped, treated screenshot (cropped to the region that tells the story, then ghosted, shadowed, highlighted or called out) may ship. Weak: an uncropped full screen pasted as the whole layout, or body text set over a busy screenshot. Never bleed text or the logo.

## Reading a direction out of the identity

Questions, not recipes. Answer them for the brief in front of you:

- **What is the one signal?** Which word, number, line or shape is the "highlighted keyword" of this feature? That is where green goes.
- **What is lit and what is dark?** In a dashboard at night, light comes from the data. What in this feature emits light; what sits back in navy?
- **What are the layers?** Front (the focal, crisp), middle (context, simplified), back (ghosted, maybe bled). Where do hairlines separate them; where does a soft shadow lift one?
- **Which ramp carries the structure, and where does the one gradient go?** Green structure with green focal, or blue structure so the green focal stands alone?
- **Is distance part of the idea?** Only then consider one blurred plane.
- **Would a stranger get it without the label?** If the stage only works with a caption, the idea isn't drawn yet.
