# Slop tells

Source: own-words digest of the installed skills `emil-unslop-design`, `emil-unslop-writing` and `emil-design-foundations` (icons-and-illustrations.md); no licence files; not copied.

The brief's brand section wins over anything here. A brand choice is never a tell (a dark-dominant brand keeps its dark ground); the tell is the unchosen, default version of it.

No single element makes a piece look machine-made; viewers recognise the pile-up of statistically common choices. Run this before hand-back and in review, in two steps: remove the tells, then restore an opinion. Never stop after step one. Why: a piece that is merely clean of tells is still generic.

## The pass

1. **Swap test**: could this piece announce another product's launch with only the logo changed? If yes, everything except the logo needs rework.
2. **Subtract first.** For every element (shape, line, badge, label, glow, word) ask what breaks if it's gone. Nothing? Delete it. Walk again; deletions expose each other.
3. **Sweep** colour, type, composition, material and effects, icons and illustration, then copy.
4. **Restore an opinion** (last section).
5. **Final question**: which element would tip a designer off that a model did this? Fix it and ask again until no element does.

## Visual tells → fix

- **Unchosen indigo, violet or purple-to-cyan** → colours from the brand, or derived from the subject.
- **Dark-and-neon as costume** (dark ground, glowing edges, neon accents to say "tech") → dark only if it's the brand; drop the glow; light from one source; the signal colour on one thing.
- **Several gradients, or one with no job** → at most one, from the palette, doing something (light falloff, distance).
- **Gradient text** → a solid colour with real contrast.
- **Glass without a reason** (frosted translucent panel over a flat ground) → a solid surface, or put something meaningful behind the glass.
- **Orbs, blobs, bokeh dots filling space** → delete; if it then feels empty, the focal subject is too weak, so strengthen that.
- **Glow as the depth cue; border + shadow + ring on one object** → one depth system (depth-and-surfaces.md).
- **Effects without a material** (grain, noise, blur, chrome applied as filters) → name the material; every effect is something that material does.
- **Blur as the depth system** (backgrounds, neighbours, half the stage out of focus) → one blurred plane at most, only when distance is the idea; draw the rest properly and let scale, overlap and contrast carry depth.
- **A generic dashboard mock as the stage** (sidebar, KPI tiles with made-up numbers, a bar chart) → draw the idea as an illustration; UI enters only as a drawn object the idea needs.
- **Centred-hero sameness** (pill badge, centred headline, subline, floating mock UI, orb behind) → the title placed as part of the composition; asymmetry the idea earns.
- **Three cards, or a bento of icon + title + two lines** → content sets the form: one thing large, unequal things given unequal space.
- **Tilted 3D screenshot floating in space** → a flat, deliberate crop of the real product at the scale that shows the point.
- **Placeholder UI** (skeleton bars, lorem, random-bar charts) → real labels and real-shaped data, or go fully abstract.
- **Everything similar in size and evenly spaced** → strong scale contrast, one focal point, deliberate negative space.
- **Fabricated proof** (invented logos, "10,000+ teams", made-up stats) → real numbers with a source, or cut.

## Icon and illustration tells → fix

- **Emoji as icons; sparkles for AI, a rocket for launch, a bolt for speed** → one icon set at one stroke weight, or no icon; show what the feature does.
- **Glossy or 3D stock icon packs, clip-art people, blob-plus-character scenes** → bespoke forms in the brand's illustration language, or none.
- **Two icon sets, or illustration strokes heavier or lighter than the icons** → one stroke system across the piece.
- **Small icons scaled up** (spindly strokes at 48px and above) → redraw at size with a heavier stroke.
- **Six colours in one illustration** → about three, from the palette.
- **Mixed perspective or mixed light direction in one scene** → one view, one light.
- **Space forced around a dropped-in illustration** → design the negative space into the illustration.

## Copy tells (headline, subtitle) → fix

- **A headline any competitor could paste** ("Build faster. Ship smarter.", "Unlock the power of your data") → name what changes, in the product's own nouns.
- **Model vocabulary**: unlock, supercharge, seamless, effortless, elevate, empower, leverage, reimagined, next-level, game-changer → the plain verb and the actual outcome.
- **"Not just X, it's Y"** → say Y.
- **Rule of three** ("Faster. Smarter. Simpler.") → count the real claims; one sharp claim beats three vague ones.
- **"Introducing X: the future of Y", "Meet X", "Say hello to X"** → a sentence about what the reader can now do.
- **An em dash or colon hinging the headline** → one plain sentence, or two.
- **A subtitle restating the headline** → cut it, or make it carry the one fact the headline lacks (how, for whom, which).
- **Unmeasured claims** ("blazing fast", "10× faster") → the number and where it comes from, or cut.
- **Title Case Headlines, emoji in copy** → sentence case (unless the brand says otherwise), no emoji.

## Put a point of view back in

- **Derive the image from the real thing**: the feature's own object, data shape or output, not a stock metaphor for it.
- **One distinctive decision, repeated across the set**: a crop habit, a scale contrast, a motif, a rule weight. Why: an odd choice held consistently is what reads as intent.
- **Let the content unbalance the layout**: an odd count, one oversized element.
- **Restraint**: the confident version has fewer effects, not more.
- **Specific over evocative** in copy: a concrete image or number, a headline with a reaction in it.
- **Point to one choice no competitor could lift unchanged.** If you can't, keep going.
