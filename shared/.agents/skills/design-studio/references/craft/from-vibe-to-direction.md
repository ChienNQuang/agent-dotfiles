# From vibe to direction

Source: own-words digest of the installed skill `emil-get-creative`, phases 1–2 (no licence file; not copied). Its generator-building phases are left out.

The brief's brand section wins over anything here.

For the creative director. Use it when the requester arrives with adjectives, a mood, "a bit like X", or says they can't picture it yet. The output is a small set of directions, each a researched visual language with its rules sorted, each feeding one seed.

## First, place the work on the brand ladder

1. **A brand guideline exists.** Directions are readings of it. Its rules are fixed and go in the brief's brand section; the vibe tells you which part of the brand to lean on. Directions differ in idea, material and composition, not in palette or typeface.
2. **A brand exists, nothing is written down.** Ask the requester before researching (brand-intake.md). Their answers become the brand section; then continue as level 1.
3. **No constraint** (a one-off, unbranded or personal piece). Free: the research is the only constraint.

## Capture the vibe verbatim

- Write down the requester's words exactly ("warm", "kind of hand-made", "like an old field manual"). Don't upgrade them to your nearest cliché. Why: translation is where a piece drifts off the feeling, and seeds quote the requester, never your paraphrase.
- Don't interrogate them into a spec. If they could name the typeface and the grid, they would have sent a spec. Ask only what the brand ladder needs.

## Research the language behind the vibe

Behind most looks is a way of making things, and the limits of that method produce the look. For each thread of the vibe, find out:

- **How was it made?** Print method, tool, medium, the technology of its era, the lighting.
- **What did making it that way do to colour, texture and layout?** How many inks, what grain, what happens where things overlap, what edges look like, where it fails.
- **What are its recurring motifs named?** With a name you can search it, brief it and have it built; without one you can only wave at it.

If the vibe has several threads, research them in parallel, one agent per thread, and spend here without guilt. Why: cheap research yields generic rules, and generic rules yield the median look the requester wants to escape.

Example: "like an old field manual" → mid-century technical manuals: offset print in one or two inks on off-white stock, orthographic line drawings with numbered callouts on leader lines, a strict column grid, small captions under each figure, slight misregistration between inks.

## Compress into rules

- **5–10 rules, each stated as a constraint** ("two inks and the paper", "where inks overlap they darken", "every object drawn in one flat view", "no edge is perfectly clean"), plus the list of named patterns.
- **Send the rules to the requester to cross out or keep, before any design starts.** Why: it is the cheapest taste checkpoint there is. A line of text costs nothing to change; finished pieces do.

## Sort the rules into buckets

- **Fixed**: brand rules (levels 1–2). They live in the brand section and are not repeated or reinterpreted in seeds.
- **Rules**: what makes it this language and never changes. They go into the seed as the material or process to execute completely, plus its `Avoid:` line (2–4 concrete failure looks, e.g. "a blue fill with white lines on top" for blueprint).
- **Modes**: distinct sub-directions inside the language (which motif leads, which part of the tradition to draw from). Each mode worth making becomes its own seed; this is usually where the directions come from.
- **Knobs**: continuous choices (scale, density, crop, amount of grain, roughness of edge). Leave them out of the seed. Why: every knob you set is a design decision taken away from the designer.

Only one mode means one direction: research another thread, or give the remaining seeds "the idea is yours". No knobs means it is a spec, not a direction: brief it for straight execution.

## Check against the brand before seeding

- At levels 1–2, a researched rule that conflicts with the brand is translated or dropped. Example: the tradition is ink on cream paper, the brand is a dark navy ground with one green signal → carry the process's behaviour (line quality, callouts, limited inks) onto the brand's ground, or drop the thread.
- A named concept is executed through its real process or dropped. A process reduced to a colour reads as a skin.
- Name each reference as a source for a quality (a process, a texture, a line), never as a layout to copy.
